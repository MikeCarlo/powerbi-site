Doing the work isn't enough — the agent has to update the second brain with what it did, and that loop is what makes the whole thing compound.

## Main Discussion

**Topic:** Is Rayfin as good as the hype, and what breaks when you try to use it at work

- **The licensing discovery.** Mike set up Claude to talk to the Power BI MCP server through a service principal, which reaches the semantic model over the XMLA endpoint — no Power BI Pro license needed. He calls it the epiphany of the week.

- **It also moves the cost off your capacity.** Four data agent queries on an F2 burned about 5,100 CUs in a few minutes. Running the same work through Claude against the XMLA endpoint cost nearly nothing on the Fabric side, shifting consumption to tokens he's already paying for.

- **The gap between idea and working thing is very short.** That's Mike's core case for Rayfin, and why he compares it to playing a strategy game. Not that it's easy — that the loop is fast.

- **He's deleting subscriptions.** An $85-per-user Dynamics license, a $20 tool, a $90 tool. He only ever used maybe 30% of each, and Fabric is already a sunk cost — so the data lands in Fabric rather than in someone else's platform.

- **Tommy's maintenance objection, and Mike's answer.** Who maintains the app? Mike's position: you and the AI. Have it document how the app works and what features exist, and fixes come at the same speed the build did.

- **Tommy's security objection stands.** A Power BI person introduces Rayfin to their company, and now IT is looking at a new public URL and a new auth path. Until this is seen in the wild in enterprise settings, he thinks you're asking for trouble.

- **Mike's counter: you already bet online.** If your organization is on SharePoint Online and publishing Power BI reports to the service, Rayfin isn't a categorically new risk — and there's a Microsoft security team behind it.

- **Don't lead with replacement.** Both agree the wrong opening move is proposing Rayfin as a replacement for existing systems. Proof of concept is the entry point.

- **Senior PMs get more powerful, not less.** The team of engineers needed to build a large application shrank. The person who can articulate business requirements precisely can now get most of the way to a working thing.

### Where it clearly beats a Power BI report

Custom interactions the visuals won't do, and deployment. Getting a Power BI report embedded into a website or mobile device is fiddly. Rayfin produces a public URL, the user signs in with Fabric, and the app works — authenticated, no embed configuration. For an intake form, a vacation tracker, or a task list, that's the whole difference.

### Adding to the failure-mode library

A workflow worth stealing from the news segment: after an agent fixes something, ask it to reflect on what it learned, distill it, and append it to a documented library of failure modes. Otherwise you pay to solve the same problem again next month.
