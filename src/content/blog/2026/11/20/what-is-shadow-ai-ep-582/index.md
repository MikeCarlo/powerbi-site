## Main Discussion

**Topic:** What is shadow AI? How AI is being used by your employees, with or without company approval.

### From shadow IT to shadow AI

Tommy opens with what he hears from organizations: more companies are allowing teams to use AI, but many people are going ahead regardless of their company's policies. The hosts recall the shadow IT pattern, where teams that couldn't get a proper database built master data management into a complex, macro-heavy Excel file that became a weak spot. Shadow AI is the next version: if Copilot isn't provided or isn't enough, people know what ChatGPT or Claude can do, and all it takes is a $20 subscription on a computer that isn't the company's.

### Power BI was shadow too

Tommy points out that ten years ago Power BI itself was shadow, and organizations struggled to get a handle on it. People always take the path of least resistance, which is why managed self-service exists in the form it does. The risk with any shadow concept is information being shared publicly, like publish to web, or the wrong information being communicated.

### Value, cost and leaders

Mike lays out the main choices organizations have, starting with Copilot for companies already invested in the Microsoft stack, and says the decision comes down to value compared to cost. In his experience, adoption takes off when leaders try it themselves; when a leader builds a first dashboard with Claude, that is the aha moment to roll it out to more people. Tommy cautions against handing everyone a Claude subscription as a temporary fix before you know what your real solution is. When the chat asks who maintains software built this way, Mike says he just tells the AI what the issue is and it gets fixed.

### Who owns the prompts?

Thinking back to early Power BI, the hosts remember the problems: multiple people loading the same table from the same database, extra load on backend servers, and nobody knowing what anyone else was building. Tommy warns that paying for personal subscriptions repeats that, and asks who owns the prompts and skills a team member builds on their own account, and what happens when they leave. At the very least, he wants a place to share skills and prompts. Mike sees the same lack of oversight and governance appearing in Claude, OpenAI and Copilot, and says that if you discover people using personal AI subscriptions, you should seriously consider giving them sanctioned tools in an IT-controlled way.

### Democratizing apps

Mike argues that in the same way Power BI spread reports to everyone, AI is democratizing apps, which is a bigger shift than report building. Tommy adds that the BI role has always been the face of both the data and the business. They agree that people who can take a problem, articulate it as a software solution and see it through to completion will be extremely powerful in their organizations, and that companies are only starting to work out how to govern agents that now build full software and write their own tests.

## Looking Forward

Find out how people in your organization are already using AI, then give them sanctioned tools and a shared place for prompts and skills, before personal subscriptions become the next round of shadow IT.
