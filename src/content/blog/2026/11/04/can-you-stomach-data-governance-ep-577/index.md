## Main Discussion

**Topic:** Can you stomach data governance? Tommy says many people who ask for governance do not know what it means, so the hosts define it before listing what makes an organization intolerant of it.

### What governance actually is

The hosts argue governance is much bigger than security, VNets and access. Tommy distills it to how an organization enables and empowers people with data, including the expectations for how data is accessed and used, who is accountable for it, and how results can be explained, with enforcement as one part.

### Budget

Tommy has never seen a governance program work without a set budget. Time from data engineers has to be allocated to support and not only new pipelines, and a center of excellence can be a federated group from several departments whose members have other day jobs.

### Direction

They point to the human side of governance, getting people moving in the same direction over time. Mike says teams spend too long on data products such as semantic models and who gets access, and skip definitions and metadata.

### Ownership

The third gap is ownership, both of definitions and change management day to day and of long-term initiatives. If a technology rollout never lands, someone should be responsible, and if it does, they should be rewarded.

### Where to start

They point to the Data Governance Institute documentation and the Microsoft adoption roadmap's maturity levels as a way to measure yourself, and suggest success needs a measure, for example counting that all 1,000 tables from a source landed in the lakehouse. Even without leadership permission, small steps such as documenting tribal knowledge with an agent can move things forward.

## Looking Forward

Start by assessing where you sit on the Microsoft adoption roadmap maturity levels, then ask what budget, direction and ownership you have today for the governance you say you want.
