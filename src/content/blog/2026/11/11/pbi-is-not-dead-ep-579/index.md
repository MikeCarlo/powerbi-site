## News & Announcements

- **[A leader in data integration, six years running](https://community.fabric.microsoft.com/blog/fbc_fabricupdatesblogs/a-leader-in-data-integration—six-years-running/5400776)**. Microsoft was named a leader in the Gartner 2026 Magic Quadrant for Data Integration Tools for the sixth consecutive year, and the post frames Fabric as a unified, AI-powered data platform. The hosts look at the other vendors on the chart, including Informatica after its acquisition by Salesforce, and are surprised by how some rank on completeness of vision.

- **[Announcing the retirement of R and Python visuals in Power BI](https://community.fabric.microsoft.com/blog/fbc_pbiupdatesblog/announcing-the-retirement-of-r-and-python-visuals-in-power-bi/5400748)**. R and Python visuals, both custom ones and those from the marketplace, will stop working in reports starting in May 2027. The hosts note they were always static images and not interactive, and Mike says an AI agent can now produce a custom visual for you instead.

## Main Discussion

**Topic:** Is Power BI dead? The hosts take the question from the title and argue about what Power BI is once Fabric, agents and apps are in the picture.

### Power BI evolved

Tommy points out how much investment went into making Power BI work well with the semantic model and says it is an ecosystem of Power Query, DAX, security, business logic and reports, not only visuals. Mike wants to cut the clicks with agents, but sees the Power BI Desktop bridge, the MCP server and Fabric apps as signs Desktop is not going away.

### Three products in one

Mike argues the old bundle of Power BI Desktop, Power Query and the semantic model has split into separate products, with the semantic model now the shining star that serves everything else. They note that most new features arrive through Fabric.

### Reports, apps and certification

Mike expects other ways to interact with data, such as Excel analysis and Fabric apps, to gradually weaken the reporting layer, though they all still use the semantic model. Tommy asks whether a certified report could ever be a Fabric app, and they discuss that certification means documented process, ownership and support that would apply to apps too.

### Naming and licensing

They end on the Fabric rebrand. Mike thinks it could have stayed Power BI with Power BI capacities absorbing the rest, and explains Fabric as Power BI plus Azure services moving into one platform. They agreed more than they argued, and say there is another hour in the topic.

## Looking Forward

Check which of your reports use R or Python visuals before May 2027, and decide what to certify, report or app, with the same process behind it.
